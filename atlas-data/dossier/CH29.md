# CH29 — Decision Memos and Personal Bylaws

*Part IV: Governing Agency · Stage: governance · Draft: Draft 1.1 — Direct address conversion (second person) 2026-08-30 (2026-08-30) · 2298 words*

## Thesis (Overview › Core Claim)

Functioning institutions make decisions through documented processes, and the reader has been operating without any of it because the cultural template never offered personal bylaws as a category worth building. The decision memo and the personal bylaw are that missing documentation practice at personal scale, and both produce compounding returns once installed.

## Five-minute summary (Overview text)

**Purpose.** Gives the reader two concrete, transferable governance tools inside Part IV: the decision memo (for novel decisions) and the personal bylaw (for recurring ones). The chapter turns the abstract case for governance built in CH22-CH28 into two documents the reader can actually write. It closes Part IV's structural teaching before CH30 walks what happens when the structure has to change.

**Reader problem.** The reader remakes the same decisions repeatedly because the previous decision was never recorded, and makes decisions under pressure that a rested version of the reader would not have made, because no prior decision exists to defer to. The chapter names both costs and gives the reader a way to stop paying them.

**Movement.** Five sections: "A Practice Most Adults Have Never Tried" (names the absence and its two costs) → "What a Decision Memo Is" (the nine memo elements) → "What a Personal Bylaw Is" (the five bylaw elements, with two worked examples: optional meetings, family loans) → "How the Practices Operate Together" (the memo-to-bylaw pipeline) → "What This Practice Protects" (decision exhaustion, reactive decision making, inconsistency), closing with the hand-off to CH30.

**Takeaway.** The reader closes the chapter able to write a decision memo for the decision currently in front of them and to identify at least one recurring decision worth turning into a bylaw, understanding both as compounding investments rather than paperwork for its own sake.

## Key concepts

- the decision memo: question, context, options, criteria, analysis, recommendation, consultation, decision, review
- the personal bylaw: decision class, default, exceptions, exception procedure, reasoning
- the memo-to-bylaw pipeline: a novel decision becomes a memo, a recurring decision becomes a bylaw, a failed bylaw becomes a new memo
- three failures the practice protects against: decision exhaustion, reactive decision making, inconsistency that erodes trust
- Bezos's six-page narrative memo discipline as precedent for the memo's structure
