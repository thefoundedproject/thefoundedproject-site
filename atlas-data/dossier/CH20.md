# CH20 — Reclaiming Choice

*Part III: Reclaiming Agency · Stage: reclamation · Draft: Draft 1.3 — Plain-language recheck, concrete grounding added, 2026-09-01 (2026-08-22) · 1816 words*

## Thesis (Overview › Core Claim)

Choice does not return through insight. It returns through repeated experience of choosing, inside conditions the body can tolerate. The reader rebuilds the choosing muscle through a small-to-medium-to-large progression, the same way mastery experience builds self-efficacy: each completed choice becomes evidence the nervous system uses to evaluate the next one.

## Five-minute summary (Overview text)

**Purpose.** Opens the practice section of Part III - Reclaiming Agency. The previous chapters walked the conditions that let choice begin returning; this chapter walks the practice of reclaiming choice itself, at the level of the body and the daily decision. It hands off to CH21, the part's closing move from survival to self-trust.

**Reader problem.** The reader may be years into a life where choosing has gone dormant, defaulting to autopilot on breakfast, clothing, the first ten minutes of the day, which conversations to enter, which commitments to keep. The chapter gives that reader a starting point small enough for the body to actually begin, rather than a call to make the large choice the reader is not yet regulated enough to sustain.

**Movement.** Six sections: "Where Choice Actually Lives" (choice as practiced capacity, not insight) → "What Small Choices Actually Look Like" (breakfast, clothing, mornings, conversations, commitments) → "What Medium Choices Actually Look Like" (declining, requesting, beginning, ending) → "What Large Choices Actually Look Like" (vocation, partnership, geography, family) → "The Trap of Performance" (visible choice without integrated capacity) → "What the Reader Can Track" (three signals, hand-off to CH21).

**Takeaway.** The reader closes the chapter with permission to start small, a concrete sense of what a small choice actually looks like this week, and a frame for why starting small is the discipline, not the consolation prize.

## Key concepts

- small, medium, and large choices, and why the sequence matters more than the size
- the choosing muscle: choice as a practiced capacity, not a decision made once
- mastery experience and self-efficacy as the mechanism underneath the sequence
- the trap of performance: making the large, visible choice before the body has the capacity to integrate it
- three trackable signals of reclamation: expanding regulatory range, emerging preference, and shifting relationships

## Sources

- **FP-S0114** [direct · journal article] Albert Bandura, "Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change," Psychological Review 84, no. 2 (1977): 191-215. Bandura identifies mastery experience, the direct memory of having succeeded at a task before, as the strongest of the four sources of self-efficacy, stronger than encouragement or observation of others. The chapter's small-to-large choice progression is a practical application of building self-efficacy through accumulated mastery experience rather than through insight or motivation alone.
