# CH35 — Rhetorical Points and the Practice of Discernment

*Part V: Protecting Agency · Stage: discernment · Draft: Draft 1.2 — Plain-language recheck (abstraction-drift fix) 2026-09-01, on top of Draft 1.1's direct-address conversion 2026-08-30 (2026-08-30) · 1773 words*

## Thesis (Overview › Core Claim)

A small set of six questions, applied consistently rather than mastered as formal argument analysis, produces discernment that compounds with use. The practice does not make the reader smarter; it gives the reader's existing intelligence better access to an information environment engineered to bypass reasoning.

## Five-minute summary (Overview text)

**Purpose.** Introduces Rhetorical Points as the Founded Project's discernment tool and hands the reader its operational core: six questions applied consistently to any claim, argument, or source. The chapter positions discernment as the protective layer that sits under everything Parts II-IV already built (agency, governance, contribution) and everything Part V continues to build.

**Reader problem.** The reader has not been taught the rhetorical literacy the current information environment requires, and that absence makes the reader more vulnerable to manipulation than the reader realizes. The chapter names the gap and hands over a usable practice rather than a taxonomy to memorize.

**Movement.** Five sections plus a hand-off: "What Rhetorical Points Was Built For" (names the gap and introduces the project) → "The Six Questions" (the practice itself) → "How the Practice Develops" (the maturation arc) → "What the Practice Does Not Do" (limits and epistemic humility) → "How the Practice Supports the Larger Architecture" (ties discernment to agency, governance, contribution, relationships) → "What the Next Chapter Walks" (hands off to GroundedVote).

**Takeaway.** The reader closes the chapter holding six portable questions they can apply today, and an understanding of why the practice is not optional: the agency, governance, and contribution the reader has already built only function correctly if the information they operate on is accurate.

## Key concepts

- the six questions: what is claimed, what evidence supports it, who benefits, what emotion is activated, what alternatives exist, what would change the reader's mind
- practice maturation over time: slow and effortful at first, then faster, then an automatic background review function
- what the practice does not do: it does not produce certainty, does not eliminate disagreement, does not make the reader smarter, does not insulate the reader from the reader's own biases, does not replace expertise
- discernment as protective infrastructure under agency, governance, contribution, and relationships

## Study prompts (Questions › Diagnostic)

- Which of the six questions do I skip most often, and what has skipping it cost me?
- Think of a claim I accepted this week without asking what evidence supports it. What was it, and why did I let it through?
- Can I currently name what would change my mind about a belief I hold strongly? If I can't, what does that tell me about whether I'm reasoning or defending?
- Where has speed beaten the practice for me, a moment I moved fast on a claim because the pace of the feed or the conversation punished slowing down?
- Who benefits if I believe the things I currently believe most strongly, and have I ever actually asked that question of my own positions?
